# SPEAK - Product Reset Plan

**Date:** 2026-08-15  
**Status:** Product and wireframing brief  
**Purpose:** Define the product before any further interface or feature development.

> This document reflects the user inputs and the product recommendations developed during the reset discussion. It is not a record that every recommendation has been approved. The wireframing process should make the proposed experience tangible, expose trade-offs, and collect decisions before implementation.

---

## 1. Product definition

SPEAK is a personal communication coach that helps Adarsh become calmer, clearer, more articulate, and more compelling in English and practical Hindi while redirecting compulsive phone use into short, meaningful spoken practice.

The product is not primarily:

- a vocabulary flashcard application;
- an infinite educational feed;
- a medical or diagnostic voice tool;
- an open-ended AI companion;
- a broad general-knowledge application.

The core learning loop is:

> **Speak -> receive one useful correction -> repeat -> hear the improvement.**

Every major product surface must support this loop or help the user enter it.

### Product promise

> Open SPEAK instead of social media, complete a useful spoken rep, and gradually become someone who speaks with calm authority, clear structure, natural vocabulary, and engaging delivery.

### Desired user identity

The app should reinforce an identity rather than merely count activity:

> "I can express what I mean clearly, calmly, and in a way people want to follow."

## 2. User inputs that shape the product

These are stated user preferences and circumstances, not inferred product choices:

- All major skills should improve together; the user does not want isolated weekly subjects.
- A normal weekday can support approximately 20 minutes of deliberate practice.
- Practice may happen at home, while walking, in a car, or wherever time is available.
- Daily recording and listening back are acceptable.
- Audio may be uploaded for meaningful AI feedback.
- A phone can be placed at a repeatable distance, approximately 30 cm, for comparable volume measurements.
- The product should both reduce total phone use and replace some unproductive phone use with learning.
- English and Hindi are separate learning needs. Hindi is primarily a practical vocabulary lane.
- General learning should enter through "teach it back," not turn SPEAK into a broad learning platform.
- Important real-life speaking contexts include:
  - telling friends an incident with clarity and the right words;
  - daily office calls;
  - explaining feelings;
  - discussing geopolitics, history, philosophy, and psychology;
  - telling a life story.
- Professional context includes product, technology, consulting, AI, and MBA-style business communication.
- Style references include:
  - Palki Sharma for composure, clarity, and controlled authority;
  - Ganesh Prasad / Think School for structured explanation and narrative logic;
  - radio jockeys for conversational hooks, flow, suspense, and memorable landings;
  - Samay Raina as a reference for contemporary, expressive, conversational Hindi rather than formal textbook Hindi.
- The user reports no pain while speaking, persistent hoarseness, swallowing difficulty, or sudden voice loss.

## 3. Problems the product must address

### 3.1 Phone behavior

- Habitual, impulsive phone checking.
- Desire to reduce overall phone time.
- Desire to redirect unavoidable phone use toward improvement.

### 3.2 Voice and delivery

- Habitually loud and effortful speech.
- Fast delivery and words merging together.
- Difficulty maintaining a quiet conversational register.
- Breath running short during speech despite normal self-administered capacity measures.
- Limited use of pause, pitch, pace, and warmth as expressive tools.
- A tendency to use volume as the main emphasis mechanism.

### 3.3 Expressive articulation

- Difficulty explaining an incident smoothly.
- Difficulty selecting the right words quickly.
- Difficulty explaining feelings precisely.
- Difficulty structuring an explanation while simultaneously managing voice and vocabulary.

### 3.4 Language

- Practical English vocabulary and retrieval.
- Words for ordinary actions and experiences.
- Natural corporate and consulting language.
- Pronunciation and word-ending clarity.
- Practical Hindi vocabulary and sentence usage without overly formal Hindi.

### 3.5 Storytelling and thought leadership

- Telling personal and life stories with flow.
- Explaining topics of interest with structure and clarity.
- Holding a listener through hooks, transitions, examples, turns, and landings.

## 4. Learning model: balanced every day, rotating emphasis

The twelve-week period is a **measurement horizon**, not a sequence in which one skill waits for another.

All major abilities appear in normal daily practice:

- voice efficiency;
- volume control;
- pace and pause;
- phonetic clarity and pronunciation;
- English vocabulary retrieval;
- practical Hindi vocabulary;
- explanation and expressive articulation;
- storytelling or workplace communication;
- feedback and repetition.

The main mission rotates so the experience remains varied and personally relevant. Rotation changes the context, not the availability of the underlying skills.

### 4.1 Standard 20-minute session

| Block | Time | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Voice reset | 3 min | Release tension, SOVT/humming, and find an easier voice |
| Volume and delivery | 3 min | Personal volume target, quiet register, pace, pause, and emphasis |
| Precision | 2 min | Pronunciation, word endings, consonant clarity, or sentence chunking |
| Vocabulary | 3 min | English retrieval plus 1-2 minutes of practical Hindi vocabulary |
| Main speaking mission | 5 min | Incident, workplace situation, feelings, teach-back, or life story |
| AI feedback and redo | 4 min | One key correction, a better structure, and a second recording |

The exact seconds may vary, but the session should remain near 20 minutes and preserve all six blocks.

### 4.2 Compound practice

The app should combine objectives instead of presenting unrelated mini-lessons.

Example:

> Explain why an AI feature should not be launched yet. Use "premature" and "trade-off." Begin at quiet conversational volume, pause before the recommendation, and finish within 60 seconds.

One rep can train vocabulary, corporate communication, explanation, pace, pause, volume, articulation, and structure.

### 4.3 Rotating main missions

- **Incident:** Tell a friend what happened and why it mattered.
- **Office:** Give an update, explain a delay, disagree, persuade, or recommend.
- **Feelings:** Name and explain an emotion without relying on vague intensifiers.
- **Teach-back:** Explain something learned about geopolitics, history, philosophy, psychology, product, technology, consulting, or AI.
- **Life story:** Tell a personal experience with a hook, turn, and landing.
- **Tone:** Deliver the same meaning as neutral, warm, reassuring, firm-but-polite, apologetic, or enthusiastic.

### 4.4 Practice environments

The session begins by asking what is possible now:

- **Can speak freely:** full recording, volume measurement, and AI feedback.
- **Can speak quietly:** quiet-register, pronunciation, vocabulary, and short recording.
- **Cannot speak now:** listening, story outlining, vocabulary retrieval, and preparation for a later spoken rep.

Silent mode supports continuity but cannot count as a completed spoken coaching loop.

## 5. Two phone-use loops

### 5.1 Deliberate daily practice

- Approximately 20 minutes.
- Designed for measurable improvement.
- Includes recording, feedback, and an immediate redo.
- May be completed in one session or resumed across blocks.

### 5.2 Impulse Rescue

When the user opens SPEAK instead of a distracting application:

1. Tap **I came here instead**.
2. Receive a 30-60 second personally relevant challenge.
3. Say or prepare one meaningful response.
4. Record **Urge redirected**.
5. Choose either **Put the phone down** or **Continue into practice**.

The app must not automatically convert Rescue into an infinite session.

### 5.3 Engagement principle

The product should be compelling through improvement, relevance, and identity - not through casino mechanics or endless consumption.

Healthy retention hooks include:

- hearing the second attempt improve;
- personally relevant scenarios;
- a growing archive of stories and explanations in the user's own voice;
- Day 1 versus current comparisons;
- increasingly realistic missions;
- visible mastery of words in spontaneous speech;
- evidence of quieter, clearer delivery;
- real-world wins recorded after meetings and conversations.

## 6. Volume and decibel measurement

### 6.1 Measurement principle

The phone microphone can measure **dBFS**, not trustworthy absolute room-level dB SPL without external calibration.

The product should communicate relative measurements such as:

- habitual baseline: 0 dB relative;
- today's session: -5.8 dB from baseline;
- personal target: approximately -6 to -8 dB from baseline;
- current state: below target, inside target, or above target;
- drift: how often and when the voice moved above the target.

It should not claim that the user spoke at a precise absolute SPL such as 72 dB unless a separately calibrated workflow is introduced.

### 6.2 Comparable measurement protocol

For the first seven comparable sessions:

1. Use the same phone and built-in microphone.
2. Place the phone approximately 30 cm from the mouth in a consistent orientation.
3. Use a reasonably quiet room.
4. Capture the room noise floor before speech.
5. Record 45-60 seconds of habitual speech.
6. Record a second sample at quiet conversational volume.
7. Record whether the browser actually disabled automatic gain control.
8. Average the habitual samples to establish the personal baseline.

### 6.3 Two measurement modes

- **Comparable mode:** same phone, distance, and broadly similar environment. Used for longitudinal trends.
- **Anywhere mode:** used in changing environments. Provides within-session feedback and drift detection but does not make strong comparisons with prior rooms.

### 6.4 Feedback behavior

- Show a calm live indicator rather than a constantly flashing meter.
- Ignore individual loud syllables.
- Nudge only when the rolling average remains outside the band.
- Encourage pauses; never classify silence as speaking too quietly.
- Store the session average and time-above-target.
- Keep MPT testing weekly rather than turning it into a daily record-chasing game.

### 6.5 Safety boundary

The app is a training tool, not a clinical diagnostic system. It should repeat the report's escalation guidance for persistent hoarseness, pain, swallowing difficulty, sudden voice loss, unexplained throat symptoms, or a lack of improvement after sustained correct practice.

## 7. Content system

The content strategy is hybrid. The app should neither hardcode everything nor generate everything live.

### 7.1 Layer A - fixed authored foundation

Prepared and reviewed in advance:

- voice exercises and safety instructions;
- calibration and retest protocols;
- story and explanation frameworks;
- workplace communication patterns;
- pronunciation patterns;
- core English vocabulary;
- practical Hindi vocabulary;
- examples of effective and ineffective responses;
- feedback rubrics.

AI must not casually alter safety-critical voice instructions.

### 7.2 Layer B - cached scenario library

Prepared ahead of use and available offline:

- product and consulting updates;
- AI and technology discussions;
- stakeholder conversations;
- delays, risks, trade-offs, recommendations, and disagreement;
- friend-to-friend incidents;
- feelings and interpersonal situations;
- geopolitics, history, philosophy, and psychology teach-back prompts;
- personal and life-story prompts.

### 7.3 Layer C - personalized daily assembly

The local engine assembles a daily session from:

- vocabulary due for recall;
- recent mistakes;
- previously captured incidents and questions;
- professional and intellectual interests;
- weaknesses found in recent recordings;
- content not practised recently;
- a rotating mission context.

Daily selection should not require a network call.

### 7.4 Layer D - AI-generated personalization

AI may:

- turn a captured incident into a future speaking mission;
- create relevant variations of a trusted scenario;
- suggest a better structure for the user's own meaning;
- generate interest-based teach-back prompts;
- personalize vocabulary usage tasks;
- construct a workplace roleplay with a role, goal, and constraint.

Frequently needed content should be generated ahead of time, verified, and cached. The app must never show an empty state because an API is unavailable.

### 7.5 Quality gate for permanent content

1. Generate candidates.
2. Verify them with a separate low-temperature model pass.
3. Reject unnatural, uncertain, duplicated, or overly formal items.
4. Tag source, seed, model, and batch.
5. Cache approved items.
6. Allow a thumbs-down to remove the item and record the pattern that failed.

This gate is mandatory for idioms, corporate language, collocations, Hindi phrasing, and permanent vocabulary examples.

### 7.6 Capture and teach-back

Capture accepts text or voice from anywhere in the app:

- "I could not explain this in today's meeting."
- "I heard the word 'nuance' but do not use it naturally."
- "I want to explain this geopolitical event."
- "Something happened today that I want to tell as a story."

The app must later show what the capture became: vocabulary card, story mission, office scenario, teach-back prompt, pronunciation rep, or discarded item.

## 8. AI and feedback architecture

### 8.1 Division of responsibility

**On-device signal analysis** owns:

- relative volume;
- volume drift;
- duration;
- phonation timing;
- basic pauses and silence;
- measurements that must work offline.

**Speech-to-text and AI** own:

- transcript;
- structure;
- word choice;
- clarity of meaning;
- filler and repetition patterns;
- whether the prompt was answered;
- a tighter version preserving the user's meaning;
- personalized follow-up prompts.

**Audio-capable AI**, when used, may add feedback on delivery, warmth, and intelligibility. It must not pretend to make clinical diagnoses.

### 8.2 Feedback contract

After a normal spoken mission, return:

1. **One win** - a specific thing that worked.
2. **One correction** - the highest-value next change.
3. **A cleaner structure** - short outline or rewritten version preserving the meaning.
4. **Redo target** - one instruction for the immediate second attempt.

Do not overwhelm the user with a long report after every recording.

### 8.3 Gemini and Groq

The existing server proxy and task allowlist are reusable foundations.

Proposed roles:

- **Gemini:** richer semantic feedback, nuanced rewrites, scenario creation, and audio-capable analysis when supported and useful.
- **Groq:** fast structured text processing, verification, generation fallback, and quota resilience.
- **Local application:** selection, scheduling, signal metrics, cached content, and offline fallback.

Provider choice must remain an implementation detail. The user experience should degrade gracefully when either API is unavailable.

### 8.4 Recording privacy

- The user has expressed willingness to upload recordings for proper feedback.
- The interface must still show when audio leaves the device.
- Local-only practice remains available.
- Retention and deletion rules must be explicit before deployment.
- API keys remain server-side.

## 9. Proposed information architecture

The existing four-tab card application should not constrain the reset wireframes.

### 9.1 Primary areas

1. **Today**
   - recommended mission;
   - 60-second, 3-minute, and 20-minute options;
   - environment choice;
   - resume an unfinished daily session.

2. **Coach**
   - structured voice and delivery session;
   - volume calibration;
   - quiet register;
   - pace, pause, pronunciation, and tone exercises.

3. **Practice**
   - incidents;
   - workplace situations;
   - feelings;
   - teach-back;
   - life stories;
   - English vocabulary;
   - Hindi vocabulary.

4. **Progress**
   - before-and-after recordings;
   - weekly comparable sample;
   - personal volume trend;
   - vocabulary demonstrated in spontaneous speech;
   - story and explanation archive;
   - real-world wins;
   - Day 1 versus current comparison.

### 9.2 Global Capture

Capture is a persistent text/microphone action available from every primary area. It is not required to occupy a permanent navigation tab.

### 9.3 Hindi placement

- Hindi remains separate from the normal English content selection.
- It receives approximately 1-2 minutes in the standard daily session.
- It is also available as a deliberate vocabulary lane inside Practice.
- Hindi uses practical conversational examples and requires spoken usage.

## 10. Progress, rewards, and measurement

### 10.1 North-star product metric

> **Completed Speak -> Feedback -> Repeat loops per week.**

A tap, a viewed card, or time spent scrolling does not count as a completed coaching loop.

### 10.2 Communication outcomes

- How often other people ask the user to repeat himself.
- Self-reported speaking effort.
- Frequency of real-world loudness drift.
- Monthly clarity, structure, and warmth comparison on a repeated 60-second task.
- Ability to retrieve and use target vocabulary after 7 and 30 days.
- Ability to tell the same incident more clearly after coaching.
- Ability to explain a complex topic within a time limit.
- Distinguishability of neutral, warm, reassuring, firm-but-polite, apologetic, and enthusiastic delivery.

### 10.3 Phone-behavior outcomes

- Urges redirected.
- Percentage of Rescue sessions that end with the phone being put down.
- Deliberate practice minutes versus passive in-app consumption.
- Frequency of voluntary return without a guilt notification.

### 10.4 Reward principles

- Reward spoken and repeated practice, not screen time.
- Do not punish missed days with a shame screen.
- Prefer weekly consistency and comeback language over brittle streak loss.
- Unlock more realistic missions through demonstrated practice.
- Use the voice and story archive as the strongest long-term reward.

## 11. Twelve-week measurement horizon

All skills begin on Day 1. The twelve weeks provide evidence of change:

- **Day 1:** comparable baseline, natural story, workplace explanation, vocabulary snapshot, and initial self-ratings.
- **Daily:** balanced practice and short feedback loops.
- **Weekly:** comparable voice sample, MPT check when due, one repeated speaking prompt, and one real-life mission review.
- **Monthly:** broader retest and a six-tone emotional-palette recording.
- **Day 84:** repeat the Day 1 tasks, compare recordings, complete a showcase story, and determine the next adaptive cycle.

Weekly mission themes may influence the main prompt, but they must not remove daily practice in the other skill families.

## 13. Delivery sequence after plan review

This is a product-delivery order, not a weekly learning curriculum.

### Stage 1 - Wireframe the learning loop

Create and test the critical flows listed in Section 14. Do not implement product code.

### Stage 2 - Build one complete vertical slice

One experience should run end to end:

> Today -> voice reset -> compound mission -> recording -> signal metrics -> AI feedback -> redo -> comparison -> progress archive.

The vertical slice should include one workplace mission, one incident/story mission, one teach-back mission, and one Hindi vocabulary rep using the same underlying interaction model.

### Stage 3 - Fourteen-day private pilot

Use the product personally before expanding it. Observe:

- whether it is opened voluntarily;
- whether recording happens in real contexts;
- whether the redo is completed;
- whether feedback is useful rather than generic;
- whether the user returns to life after Rescue;
- whether the 20-minute routine feels coherent.

### Stage 4 - Personalization and content operations

- weakness profile;
- capture-to-mission processing;
- verified content generation;
- adaptive daily assembly;
- interest-based teach-back;
- improved longitudinal comparisons.

### Stage 5 - Advanced interaction

Only after the core loop proves useful:

- scenario-bound live voice partner;
- richer audio analysis;
- advanced workplace roleplay;
- deeper story coaching;
- carefully justified reminders and widgets.

## 14. Wireframing handoff instructions

### 14.1 Source-of-truth rule

Use this document as the product brief. Review the existing application only to identify reusable logic and current problems. Do not preserve its feed, tabs, card anatomy, or roadmap merely because they already exist.

### 14.2 Required flows

Wireframe these flows before designing a complete screen inventory:

1. **First-run and baseline**
   - purpose and expectation;
   - speaking environment;
   - microphone permission and self-test;
   - 30 cm placement guidance;
   - habitual and quiet samples;
   - natural story or explanation;
   - starting profile without diagnostic language.

2. **Today**
   - 60-second, 3-minute, and 20-minute choices;
   - recommended mission;
   - environment selector;
   - resume state;
   - clear single primary action.

3. **Twenty-minute coaching session**
   - all six daily blocks;
   - progress without feeling like a long form;
   - pause/resume;
   - transition from drills to the main mission.

4. **Record -> Feedback -> Redo**
   - recording state;
   - live relative-volume feedback;
   - upload/processing state;
   - one win, one correction, cleaner structure, redo target;
   - side-by-side or sequential comparison;
   - save best attempt.

5. **Impulse Rescue**
   - one-tap entry;
   - 30-60 second challenge;
   - Urge redirected confirmation;
   - Put the phone down as a first-class action;
   - optional continuation into practice.

6. **Capture -> Future mission**
   - text and voice capture;
   - saved confirmation;
   - visible processing outcome;
   - approve, edit, or discard generated mission.

7. **Progress and voice archive**
   - Day 1 versus current;
   - weekly comparable samples;
   - personal volume trend;
   - completed feedback-redo loops;
   - vocabulary used spontaneously;
   - real-world wins.

8. **Practice selection**
   - incident, office, feelings, teach-back, life story, tone, English vocabulary, and Hindi vocabulary;
   - avoid a dense catalogue or card-grid feel;
   - show why a practice is recommended.

### 14.3 Mobile constraints

- Primary frame: 375 x 812.
- Designed for one-handed use.
- Every primary tap target at least 44 x 44.
- The prompt and recording action remain visible without hunting.
- No horizontal scrolling.
- Recording and microphone state must always be unambiguous.
- The interface must work with changing ambient noise and denied microphone permission.
- Avoid displaying more than one coaching correction at a time.
- Avoid long instructional paragraphs during an active voice exercise.

### 14.4 States that must be wireframed

- first visit;
- microphone denied;
- automatic gain control not disabled;
- noisy environment;
- offline practice;
- AI unavailable or quota exhausted;
- upload in progress;
- feedback failed but local metrics succeeded;
- interrupted session and resume;
- user cannot speak now;
- calibration incomplete;
- comparable sample due;
- Day 1 with no progress history;
- mature user with twelve weeks of recordings.

### 14.5 Content examples to use in wireframes

Use realistic personal scenarios rather than lorem ipsum:

- Tell a friend about a confusing incident at work.
- Explain why an AI feature launch is premature.
- Give a concise product-status update.
- Explain disappointment without saying "very upset."
- Teach back a geopolitical event in 60 seconds.
- Tell a life story using Hook -> Context -> Turn -> Landing.
- Use a practical Hindi word in a natural conversational sentence.

### 14.6 Required wireframing output

The wireframing chat should produce:

1. A short experience strategy.
2. A proposed navigation model with rationale.
3. Flow diagrams for the eight required flows.
4. Low-fidelity mobile wireframes.
5. State and edge-case wireframes.
6. A component inventory.
7. Questions or trade-offs exposed by the wireframes.

It should not start production implementation until the wireframes have been reviewed.

## 16. Definition of a successful reset

The reset succeeds when the product is easy to explain in one sentence and the primary experience proves the learning loop:

> "I opened SPEAK, said something meaningful, received one correction, repeated it better, and either continued deliberately or put my phone down."

If the wireframes cannot make that experience obvious, the product is still too mixed and should not move into implementation.
