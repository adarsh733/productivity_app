01What's wrong with it today

Before drawing anything I went back through the shipped Phase 0 interface. Twelve things, most of them structural rather than cosmetic. The redesign below is answering this list, item by item — if you disagree with a row, say so and the wireframe that depends on it changes.

What it does nowWhat the wireframes do instead
1Near-black shell (#0b0d10) with seven saturated hues on top. Warm paper (#FBFAF8), white cards, near-black ink. Light is the theme, not an option — no toggle, no dark variant to maintain. The seven hues survive as a 3px spine and a kicker colour only.
2Four grade buttons under every card — Again / Hard / Good / Easy. That's Anki's UI, and you can't honestly answer "was that Hard or Good?" about a rep you just spoke. Two buttons: Again and Got it. Easy is a long-press. Hard was kept (decision, 2026-08-12) and becomes swipe-left — it keeps the grade without spending a fourth button on it.
3Every card renders as the same undifferentiated stack: heading, paragraph, list, prompt. A fixed anatomy where the thing you must say aloud is always the same block, in the same place, in the same colour — the bottom of the card, pinned, never scrolled off.
4"Felt the pull" sits top-right, competing with the streak, and the label doesn't say what it does. Moves to a quiet chip below the actions: "I felt the pull" → taps to "Counted." It is a confession, not a header control; it should feel private.
5Tab glyphs are ◈ ＋ अ ◉. Those read as missing icons. Four drawn line icons, 22px, plus labels. Same four tabs.
6Progress leads with a giant number that is 0 for the first week. Day one shows a starting state, not a zero. The hero stat only becomes the hero once it has earned it.
7No first-run screen at all — the app opens straight onto a breath drill with no context. Three panels, about fifteen seconds, ending inside Core 1. It sets the one expectation that matters: you will be speaking out loud.
8On a long word card, header + progress bar + body + four buttons + hint means the say-prompt can fall below the fold. The card body scrolls inside a fixed frame. Chrome is 44+38 top and 54+34+64 bottom; the say-block is pinned above the actions. The mandatory part cannot be pushed off-screen.
9Hindi is a prev/next carousel that greys out its arrows at the end. A dead end — the one thing the whole app forbids. Hindi gets the same endless queue, entered on purpose, with its own counter.
10"swipe up to pass" is permanent text at the bottom of every card, forever. Shown on the first three cards ever, as a moving hint, then never again. A permanent instruction is an admission the gesture isn't discoverable.
11Everything is one system font at three sizes, centre-ish, evenly spaced. A serif for the word itself, sans for all chrome. That single split is what stops it looking like a flashcard app and starts it looking like a dictionary you'd want to open.
12Core 3 progress is a full-width bar that scales 0 → 1. A bar implies a loading screen. Three dots. Three is a number you can hold in your head; a bar at 33% is not.

The serif is a system stack — ui-serif, "Iowan Old Style", Charter, Palatino, Georgia — so it costs zero bytes and renders natively on the iPhone. It is used for exactly one thing: the item being learned. Never for UI, never for body copy, never for numbers in the stat grid.

Space, shape, elevation

4812 162024 3248

Shape

  • 28px — the card. Nothing else uses it.
  • 18px — sheets, panels, the inbox field.
  • 10px — chips, small tiles.
  • pill — every button, every counter.
  • 20px gutter on every screen edge. One value, no exceptions.

Elevation — light theme rules

  • On paper, shadow does the work borders did on dark: card = 1px hairline + a wide, very soft shadow at 6% opacity.
  • Never a grey fill to signal "raised" — that reads as disabled on light.
  • The primary button is the only element with a coloured shadow.
  • No shadow anywhere on the tab bar. A hairline only.

03Shell & navigation

Four tabs, unchanged from Phase 0 — the change is that they're drawn rather than typed. Feed stays mounted behind the others so switching tabs never restarts a session.

Chrome budget · 375 × 812

  1. 44px status bar / safe-area top. Never drawn by us.
  2. 38px feed header — Core dots left, streak right. The streak is the only heat-coloured thing on the screen, and it disappears entirely at zero rather than showing "0-day".
  3. ~530px card. This is 65% of the screen and it should be: it is the product.
  4. 54px action row. Two targets, both ≥ 44px, thumb-reachable.
  5. 34px sub-actions — the pull chip and the swipe hint, both at ink-3. Deliberately the quietest thing on screen.
  6. 64px tab bar + safe-area bottom.
  7. Removed: the full-width progress bar, two of the four grade buttons, the permanent "swipe up to pass" caption, the header urge button.

04Card anatomy & gestures

The seven card types differ wildly in content — a breath drill and a swap have nothing in common. What makes the feed feel like one app is that they share a skeleton, and the skeleton puts the spoken part in the same place every single time.

The skeleton

  1. Spine. 3px, the type hue, inset from the corners. It is how you know at a glance that three swaps in a row happened — which is a bug you'd otherwise never see.
  2. Kicker. 11px, .16em tracking, type hue. Takes a qualifier after a middot (PHRASE · CORPORATE) so corporate idioms don't need their own screen.
  3. The item. Serif, 36px, or 28px when it's a phrase. This is the only serif on the screen and the eye lands here first.
  4. Support. Meaning, then examples with the target word underlined in accent-soft. Examples get a left rule, not bullets — they're quotes, not a list.
  5. The say-block. Pinned to the bottom of the card, tinted, never scrolls away, always says SAY THIS. If a card can be answered in silence, the card is broken — this block is how that rule gets enforced visually.
  6. Two actions. Again (112px, quiet) and Got it (fills the rest, accent). Reachable with a thumb without regripping.
  7. Sub-actions. The pull chip and the swipe hint. Both are ink-3; neither ever competes with the primary.

Decision that needs your yes

  • “Hard” disappears from the UI. The contract keeps all four grades, and the scheduler is untouched — but nothing in the interface emits hard any more. Two reasons: after speaking a sentence aloud you genuinely cannot tell Hard from Good, so the button collects noise; and a four-way choice after every card is the tax that makes Anki feel like homework. If you want hard kept, the cheapest honest version is swipe left = Hard, invisible until you discover it. Say which.

05The seven card types

Same skeleton, seven fills. Content below is real — taken from src/content/seed/00-exemplars.json — so these are honest about length. Chrome above and below is identical on all seven and is drawn once here per frame.

Content problems the wireframes exposed

  • Breath instructions are too long to read while breathing. The MPT drill ships with five steps, one of them 24 words. On the frame above I cut it to four short ones and moved "log the seconds" into the button. The seed content needs an editing pass for this — the card is read once and then you're busy.
  • Idiom cards carry a scenario and an example that say nearly the same thing. The wireframe demotes scenario to a small "when you'd use it" line and makes the say-prompt derive from it.
  • Nothing in the seed set has a "best" to beat. Breath cards need bestMptSec / best count surfaced on the card, which means the feed hook has to read it — a small change to useFeed, not the contract.

06The session arc

First open → Core 3 → the handoff → endless. The handoff is the most important screen in the app: it is the exact moment you either keep going or open Instagram.

07Capture

Used half asleep, with one thumb, in the dark. Every control that isn't the field is a reason not to bother.

Changes from the shipped screen

  1. A title and a sub-line. The bare textarea gave no reason to write anything; the question is the prompt.
  2. The field is a card, not a form control — 17px minimum so iOS never zooms on focus.
  3. Voice capture is the Phase-2 slot here, and the layout leaves room for it: a mic button pairs with Save. Speaking a thought at 3AM beats typing it, and it feeds the same classifier.
  4. The waiting list is quiet and secondary — hairline rows, ink-2, no cards. You are not meant to re-read it; you're meant to dump and close.
  5. Discard is a × per row with a 44px target and no confirm dialog. It's a scratch pad.

08हिंदी

Locked decision: Hindi never enters the main feed. But it gets the same engine — an endless queue, not a carousel with disabled arrows at the end.

Decisions this makes

  1. No prev/next. Same two actions as the feed, same swipe, same scheduler. One engine, two decks.
  2. The UI strings on this screen are Hindi. If the section is Hindi, the buttons are Hindi — English chrome around Devanagari content is the tell that it was bolted on.
  3. Its own counter, its own streak, and neither touches Core 3. Stated on the screen so it never feels like skipping Hindi costs you the streak.
  4. The serif exception is documented in tokens: --serif applies to :lang(en) terms only.

09You

Two frames: day 1 and day 40. The shipped screen shows the same layout on both, which means the first week of using the app is a wall of zeroes.

Rules for this screen

  1. Nothing here says “0”. A stat with no data shows an em-dash or a sentence. Zeroes on a progress screen are a reason to close the app.
  2. Deltas, not just totals. 16.8s +4.1 is the sentence “your breath got better”, which is the actual reason the app exists.
  3. The day grid stops at five weeks. It fits without scrolling and the recent past is the only past that changes behaviour.
  4. Grace days are visible. The streak rule allows two a month; if the app quietly forgives you, you learn nothing from the streak.
  5. Backup state moves to a single line at the bottom, and only appears when sync is not configured.

10Empty & edge states

Every one of these currently either doesn't exist or is a centred grey sentence. On a light theme they need drawing properly, because there's nowhere for a bare sentence to hide.

Must exist

  • Queue exhausted — genuinely no cards due and none new. Never a dead end: offer a review of the ten hardest, or a breath drill. Copy: “Nothing new is due. Want the ten you find hardest?”
  • Loading — first launch seeds 370 cards. A skeleton card, not a spinner. Spinners on white read as broken.
  • Offline — the app is local-first so it just works; the only surface is a one-line note on You if sync is stale.
  • TTS unavailable — “Hear it” hides rather than failing on tap. iOS Safari with no voices installed is real.
  • Mid-card tab switch — the card is preserved exactly, including a running timer. Already fixed once; the wireframes assume it stays fixed.

Deliberately absent

  • No “you're done” screen. Anywhere. Not after Core 3, not after 60 cards, not at midnight.
  • No streak-broken screen. The number just changes. A screen mourning a lost streak is an invitation to quit.
  • No settings tab. Anything configurable in Phase 0 belongs at the bottom of You.
  • No sign-in. Supabase is backup, not an account gate — it stays a single line at the bottom of You until Phase 3.
  • No badges, no confetti, no “level 4”. The streak and the urge count are the only two rewards, and both are real.

11Motion & the rules that survive

Motion

  • Card leaves upward, 220ms, cubic-bezier(.22,.61,.36,1), fading as it goes. The next card is already behind it — no gap, ever.
  • Drag follows the thumb with 0.6 resistance, capped at 160px. Past 70px the card lightens slightly so the commit point is felt, not guessed.
  • Core dot fills with a 120ms scale pop. It is the only celebratory motion in the app.
  • Swipe hint animates on the first three cards ever, then never again.
  • prefers-reduced-motion kills all of it, and swipe still works.

Unchanged from the current build

  • Core 3 = breath → say-it → word, in that order, and only that counts for the streak.
  • Endless never ends; no dead ends anywhere.
  • IndexedDB is the read path; Supabase is backup only.
  • Hindi is never in the main feed.
  • The contract in src/types/contract.ts — the redesign is presentation-only apart from the two flagged items (grades, and reading “best” onto breath cards).
  • Every tap target ≥ 44px. The gutter is 20px everywhere.

12Answered — 2026-08-12

All four were answered. They are locked in PLAN.md §8 and written into the build brief at .claude/briefs/AG-002-phase0.5-ui-rebuild.md. Nothing here is open.

1 · Does “Hard” die?

Answer: keep it. Per the fallback stated when this was asked, hard becomes swipe-left. The button row stays at two — Again and Got it — with easy as a long-press. Four grade paths, two of them gestures.

2 · Serif for the words — yes or no?

Answer: yes. --serif is used for exactly one thing: the item being learned. Never for UI, never for body copy, never for numbers.

3 · Green accent, or something else?

Answer: sky blue, not green. The accent is #0369A1, with #E0F2FE as the say-block fill and #0EA5E9 for non-text use only (focus rings, active indicators — it is too light to carry white text). The frames below are drawn from these variables and are already correct. Two card-type hues moved away from the accent so a 3px spine is never mistaken for a call to action: word #1D4ED8 → #1E3A8A and pronounce #0E7490 → #0F766E.

4 · “Inbox” → “Capture”?

Answer: yes. A label change only — the tab, the heading, the placeholder, the empty state. File names, the route key and everything under src/features/inbox/ keep their current names.

Status: building. tokens.css is written and is the contract. The rest — components.css rewritten, the five screen components re-laid out, plus the first-run screen, drawn icons, Core dots, card actions and the gesture hook — is briefed as AG-002. The logic layer — srs/, features/, db/ — is not touched, so the 44 tests stay green throughout.
